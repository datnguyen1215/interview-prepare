Option 1: Interview room

Recommended
  • Keeps the Studio layout. The sidebar becomes the interviewer panel: the current question pinned at the top, the transcript below, and push-to-talk at the bottom.
  • Phase bar plus timer across the top keeps your pacing visible (requirements → high level → deep dive → wrap-up).
  • Trade-off: busier than a real call, but you can always re-read a question you missed hearing.

Option 2: Video call

  • Looks like a real Zoom interview: your shared screen fills the frame, with the interviewer and your camera as small tiles. Whoever is talking gets a green outline.
  • Only the current line shows, as captions. The full transcript is hidden in a drawer.
  • Trade-off: the most realistic pressure, but no visible pacing and it's easy to lose the thread.

Option 3: Coached practice

  • Three columns: problem brief and a live rubric on the left, your screen in the middle, the transcript on the right.
  • The rubric ticks off as you cover topics, and a yellow nudge appears when you skip something (e.g. "No numbers yet").
  • Trade-off: the best for learning, but the rubric gives away what the interviewer wants, so it doesn't feel like a test.

Option 4: Voice first

  • One large orb that pulses blue when the interviewer speaks and green when you do. The current question is in big type, with your live words below it.
  • Your screen recording runs as a small thumbnail. Built for behavioural rounds and talking through designs out loud.
  • Trade-off: the calmest and most focused, but bad for whiteboarding because the diagram is tiny.
